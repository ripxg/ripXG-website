The fastest way to burn through Claude's usage limit is letting context bloat unchecked.

Every session accumulates tokens: earlier messages, tool outputs, reasoning steps. By the time you're deep in a task, a large chunk of your allocation is just history. Most of it irrelevant to what you're doing right now.

Here's a skill you can add to Claude Code today that fixes this.

## Strategic Compact

The [Strategic Compact skill](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/tree/main/skills/strategic-compact) tracks your tool call count and suggests running `/compact` at natural breakpoints. After 50 tool calls, Claude will prompt you to compact the session. Every 25 calls after that, it reminds you again.

Drop the skill into `~/.claude/skills/strategic-compact/` and register it in `~/.claude/settings.json` under `hooks.PreToolUse` with a matcher for `Edit` and `Write` tool calls, pointing to `suggest-compact.sh` as the command. The [full config is in the repo](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/tree/main/skills/strategic-compact).

Running `/compact` summarises the current session and clears the context window. You keep the plan. The noise disappears.

The key is timing. Compact after finishing a debugging session, before starting a new feature. After exploration, before implementation. Not mid-task — you'll lose context you still need. At natural transition points, you drop the stale history and move forward clean.

## Why this matters

Context is not the enemy. Stale context is.

Most people try to keep prompts short to save tokens, which usually backfires. You leave out constraints, get an incomplete answer, and go back to clarify. Three calls instead of one. The fix is better-structured prompts with full context up front — but that only works if you're not carrying 80k tokens of old conversation into each new call.

Compact at boundaries. Front-load context in each prompt. Stop re-explaining your stack from scratch each session.

Those three habits cut usage more than any plan upgrade will.
